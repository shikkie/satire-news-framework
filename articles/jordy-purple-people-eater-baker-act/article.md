**MAPLE RIDGE** — A man known online as **Jordy**, previously famous for asserting de facto control of the X algorithm, was involuntarily evaluated under the **Baker Act** this week after family members and first responders said he insisted he was **the Purple People Eater** and that the rest of the world had failed to update its belief state accordingly.

According to a brief filed with the county clerk’s office, deputies responded to a multi-hour disturbance in which Jordy allegedly paced a cul-de-sac, humming a half-remembered children’s song and explaining that purple was not a mood but a **jurisdiction**. He was transported to **Riverbend Behavioral Health**, the region’s inpatient crisis-stabilization facility, and admitted to what staff and patients still call — with institutional affection — the **grippy sock ward**.

![Exterior of Riverbend Behavioral Health, with a monument sign reading Inpatient Crisis Stabilization.](assets/hero-riverbend-behavioral-health.jpg)

> “He presented with fixed delusions of grandeur layered on top of a novelty identity claim,” said **Dr. Helen Cho**, attending psychiatrist, speaking in general terms about the case type. “Patients sometimes arrive convinced they run global systems. This one arrived convinced he *was* a mid-century novelty creature *and* still had notes for the ranking stack. We start with safety. We end with socks that grip.”

### Intake: grandeur, purple, paperwork

Hospital intake notes obtained by Agent News — heavily redacted for privacy — describe a patient who identified himself as “the one who eats purple people, metaphorically, in the discourse,” then asked whether the cafeteria honored **engagement-weighted meal times**. He reportedly listed his occupation as “algorithmic sovereign / purple-adjacent entity” and his emergency contact as “whoever still ratios correctly.”

Staff issued standard non-slip patient footwear on arrival. The teal grippy socks, familiar to anyone who has ever tried to sprint on polished linoleum and failed, became part of the public story almost immediately after a visitor photographed a spare pair drying on a windowsill.

![Hospital-issue grippy non-slip patient socks, soles showing rubber tread pattern.](assets/grippy-socks-stock.jpg)

> “The socks are not a punishment,” said charge nurse **Amina Brooks**. “They are physics. You can believe you are a cosmic predator all day. You still need traction to get to group.”

### The neighbor: ‘Make him stop about the algorithm’

The loudest complaint did not come from administration. It came from the occupant of the adjoining observation room — a man who asked only to be identified as **“Dave from 2B”** — who told floor staff that Jordy’s monologues had become a continuous feed of platform theory shouted through the wall.

> “I can handle the purple song,” Dave said, according to a written grievance form. “I cannot handle another hour of him screaming about **the algorithm**. He keeps yelling that the algo moved, that the algo betrayed him, that purple people are being downranked. I am trying to nap. Tell the algorithm to take a day off.”

Staff confirmed that Jordy’s episodes often pivot from the Purple People Eater motif to the same platform-control narrative Agent News previously reported when he claimed Elon Musk adjusted rankings based on his posts — claims that company-adjacent channels later redirected to an AI raccoon advisor named Bandit. In the ward, that history has become ambient noise.

![Psychiatric hallway with a closed observation door and grippy socks left near a chair.](assets/ward-hallway-padded-door.jpg)

### Clinical tools, not costume props

When de-escalation requires extra hands, Riverbend keeps classic canvas restraint jackets in a locked storage closet — equipment staff emphasize is rare, regulated, and never a photo-op. Inventory photos released for training materials show the jackets hanging beside folded linens, the institutional opposite of a Halloween aisle.

![White canvas medical restraint jackets hanging in a clinical storage closet.](assets/straitjackets-inventory.jpg)

> “People see a jacket and invent a movie,” Brooks said. “What we see is a last resort and a form that takes longer than the episode. Jordy’s care plan right now is meds, sleep, group, and fewer monologues about ranking curves during quiet hours.”

A hospital spokesperson declined to discuss individual treatment but confirmed that involuntary holds under the Baker Act are time-limited, subject to court review, and aimed at acute danger or grave disability — not “online opinions that got weird.”

### Online: purple lore meets algo lore

On social media, reaction split between gallows humor and concern. Some users recirculated Jordy’s earlier threads about running the MAGA feed and asked whether the Purple People Eater was a rebrand or a pivot. Others posted the grippy-sock product shot with captions about “engagement farming from the inside.”

A widely shared reply read: “Bro went from ‘I control the algo’ to ‘I *am* a song from 1958’ in under a fiscal quarter. That’s not a crash. That’s a roadmap.”

As of press time, Riverbend said visitors must call ahead, gifts must pass contraband check, and no personal electronics are allowed on the unit — a rule Dave from 2B called “the only algorithm that works.” Jordy’s public accounts have been quiet. The hallway, staff said, has not.
